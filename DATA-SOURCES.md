# Tops: terminology and classification notes

This is an expandable writing vocabulary, not a complete taxonomy of world dress. Base entries distinguish cuts, construction, and garment types rather than adding color variants. Presentation and occasion tags are picker choices, not historical rules. A coordinated draw does not guarantee a culturally or historically matched ensemble.

## Japanese entries

There are 24 Japanese or explicitly Japanese-inspired tops. The catalog distinguishes recognizable garment names from modern fashion references:

- **Samue top and jinbei top:** only the upper component of each two-piece outfit. Work and lounge tags follow their different uses. [Japan Objects' garment collection](https://shop.japanobjects.com/collections/all) identifies samue jacket/trouser and jinbei shirt/short sets; [Musubi Kyoto's samue/jinbei guide](https://musubi-kyoto.jp/en/blogs/trivia/20220520samue) explains the two forms.
- **Judogi, karategi, kendogi, aikidogi, and kyudogi tops:** upper training garments, assigned to Activewear rather than complete suits. [Seido's aikido jacket description](https://www.seidoshop.com/products/deluxe-aikidogi-single-layer-as200-jacket) illustrates the jacket distinction and relationship to judo clothing. The [All Japan Kendo Federation](https://www.kendo.or.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/guidelines_for_keiko_revised-en_20210621.pdf) names kendo-gi separately from hakama.
- **Kariyushi shirt:** Okinawan shirt vocabulary; the [Okinawa clothing manufacturers' association](https://www.kariyushi-oki.com/kariyushi.php) and [Shuri Ryusen](https://shop.shuri-ryusen.net/en/products/kariyushi_co) describe the regional designation.
- **Hadajuban shirt and hanjuban top:** short underlayers only, placed in the app's lounge/underlayer pool. This placement does not mean they are traditional sleepwear. [Bokunan-do identifies hanjuban as a half-length undergarment](https://www.shop-japan.co.jp/english-boku/underwear-men-colours.htm). Full-length nagajuban are not tops.
- **Sailor-uniform blouse, knit school vest, streetwear, Lolita and Ouji fashion:** contemporary fashion references, not traditional dress. Kimono-sleeve and origami-inspired blouses are labeled modern cuts; they are not renamed kimono.

**Garment boundaries:** kimono and kosode are robes, not shirts. See the [Met's kosode entry](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/901856). Haori are outer jackets, as identified in the [Met's haori entry](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45403). Haori, hanten, happi, full kimono, yukata, and complete samue/jinbei sets were therefore not added to the Top slot. The Japanese-filter extension adds kimono and yukata to One-piece, haori/hanten/happi to Outerwear, samue trousers/jinbei shorts/divided hakama to Bottom, and geta/zori to Footwear. [JNTO’s kimono/yukata guide](https://www.japan.travel/en/sg/guide/guide-enjoy-kimono-and-yukata-experience/) and [ryokan guide](https://faq.japan-travel.jnto.go.jp/en/guide/japanese-ryokan/) provide context for robes and footwear. These collection tags are explicit metadata; untagged generic garments are not silently treated as Japanese.

## International examples

The list includes both named traditional garments and clearly labeled modern/regional-inspired designs. English glosses identify the upper-body item rather than assigning a whole ensemble to one slot.

- **Jeogori:** the upper garment in hanbok. [Visit Seoul's hanbok guide](https://english.visitseoul.net/tours/the-hanbok-en/ENN011852) describes use by men and women.
- **Kebaya:** a front-opening blouse with traditions across several Southeast Asian countries, not the property of just one country. [UNESCO's multinational entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/kebaya-knowledge-skills-traditions-and-practices-02090) documents that range. [Singapore's National Heritage Board catalog](https://www.nhb.gov.sg/-/media/nhb/files/media/releases/2023/media-release---4-apr-kebaya-media-event.pdf) describes the panel-front kotabaru form.
- **Baju kurung and baju Melayu tops:** only the shirt/tunic, not the complete outfit. [Roots Singapore](https://www.roots.gov.sg/ich-landing/ich/making-and-wearing-of-baju-kurung) explains the ensemble components.
- **Barong Tagalog:** Philippine formal-shirt vocabulary. [Asian Art Museum's garment entry](https://searchcollection.asianart.org/objects/15483/mans-shirt-barong-tagalog).
- **Kurta and choli:** South Asian shirt/tunic and fitted-blouse vocabulary. [British Museum kurta entry](https://islamicworld.britishmuseum.org/collection/RRM47386/) and [V&A Indian textiles](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/indian-textiles/).
- **Kaba blouse:** the upper component of a blouse-and-skirt outfit. [Met collection commentary](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/155862).
- **Guayabera:** Caribbean/Latin American shirt vocabulary, without asserting a single disputed birthplace. [Smithsonian garment entry](https://www.si.edu/object/guayabera%3Anmah_1374905).
- **Huipil:** this catalog explicitly chooses a blouse-length form. [Smithsonian huipil entry](https://collections.si.edu/search/detail/edanmdm%3Achndm_1960-88-3).
- **Mola-panel blouse:** the blouse incorporating the textile panels, rather than a loose panel treated as a garment. [University of Connecticut's museum explanation](https://benton.uconn.edu/exhibitions/molas/what-is-a-mola/).
- **Romanian-Moldovan shoulder-panel embroidery:** regional blouse vocabulary. [Moldova's intangible heritage inventory](https://www.patrimoniuimaterial.md/en/node/310).

The references support terminology and garment boundaries. Individual material variants, modern silhouettes, fantasy designs, and picker tag choices are authored for this app rather than reproduced from museum catalogs.

## Bottoms expansion

The Bottom slot contains lower-body separates. Layered skirt/underskirt descriptions are a single lower-body result; complete dresses and two-piece outfits are excluded. Appearance and occasion tags are editorial choices, with broadly shared trousers, skirts, and shorts available across presentations.

- **Hakama and monpe:** the collection keeps divided hakama and adds an undivided skirt form, monpe work trousers, and explicitly labeled modern/fantasy adaptations. [The Met's hakama record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/44633) documents a trouser form; [Nippon.com's hakama guide](https://www.nippon.com/en/guide-to-japan/cs00071/) provides context; [Japan Objects' pants collection](https://shop.japanobjects.com/collections/mens-pants) includes monpe. Modern workwear-inspired pieces do not claim to be historical costume.
- **Salwar:** lower-body trousers, rather than the complete salwar-kameez ensemble. [Smithsonian salwars entry](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1761681).
- **Baji and chima:** Korean trousers and skirt respectively, rather than a full hanbok. [Visit Seoul's hanbok guide](https://english.visitseoul.net/tours/The-Hanbok-EN_/11852).
- **Mamianqun:** a pleated Chinese skirt. [British Museum garment record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_2022-3030-5).
- **Sarong:** wrapped lower-garment vocabulary. [Smithsonian sarong record](https://www.si.edu/object/sarong%3Achndm_1969-50-2).

Every occasion has Japanese-tagged bottom choices for each presentation. The filter continues to exclude untagged garments even if similar items could also be worn in Japan. Coordinated mode still matches occasion, not a specific historical period, sporting uniform, or cultural ensemble.
